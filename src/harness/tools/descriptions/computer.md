Look at and act on any app on this Mac the way a person does — by seeing what is on screen and using a pointer and a keyboard. This is how you do the things that have no API and no command line. Everything happens on the user's own machine, aimed at one specific app, so it never disturbs what they are doing elsewhere. For anything on the web, use the `browser` tool instead — it reads a page's own content without the browser's chrome.

**Look with `observe`.** It reads an app's controls and text as a list of elements, each with a short stable `ref` you act on, plus its role, name, and value. A deep area comes back as a region with a count — observe it again by its ref to look inside. Observe also tells you the app's other windows and what else is currently open, so you always know where you are and what you could switch to. Every acting call re-reads the app and tells you what changed, so you rarely need to observe again just to see an effect. Refs stay valid across calls, so you can act on one you saw a few steps ago.

**Act with the pointer and keyboard.** `pointer` clicks, double-clicks, right-clicks, hovers, or drags an element — always by its `ref`, never by guessing screen coordinates. `press` sends a key or a shortcut: Return, Tab, Escape, the arrows, or a chord such as Cmd+C, Cmd+A, Cmd+F — even Cmd+Tab to move between apps. `type` enters text into a field. `select` and `caret` choose text and place the cursor by *what the text says*, so you never nudge one key at a time. `scroll` brings off-screen content into view (give it an element to reveal, or a direction).

**Combine these however the task needs — there is no fixed procedure.** Copying is just pressing Cmd+C; replacing a few words is selecting them and typing over; running a command that has no visible button is opening the menu bar (observe it with `window` set to `menu`, then click the item) or pressing its shortcut. If you get stuck in one app, get out the way you would as a person — switch with Cmd+Tab, or open the app you want from the terminal through the `bash` tool. When something you do changes nothing on screen, don't repeat it; look again and try a different way in.

**A screenshot is the last resort, and only for seeing — never for acting.** If an app exposes nothing to read (a game, a custom-drawn canvas) or a field has no accessible cursor, take a `screenshot` to see it, then tell the user what to click — do not guess coordinates and click blind. If an app simply cannot be read, say so and ask the user to do the step.

Launching or switching apps, and scripting a cooperative app for its data, are terminal one-liners — do those through the `bash` tool on this same Mac. This tool is for driving the UI you are looking at.
