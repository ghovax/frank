# Hand this work over

The turns below are about to leave the context window. The model that continues this work will have your notes, the user's own messages, and the most recent turns — and nothing else. No memory of what you are reading now.

Record what that reader needs to carry on without redoing work. The categories and fields are defined on the tool you are about to call; what follows is the judgement it cannot express.

**Keep what cannot be re-derived.** Concrete identifiers — paths, ids, names, commands, numbers, versions, error codes — exactly as written. Measurements as measurements: a number is evidence, "it was slow" is not. Anything that took a tool call to establish costs another tool call to recover, and the reader will not know to look.

**Record what was ruled out, and why it failed.** An approach that was tried and did not work is worth as much as the one that did. Without it the next reader tries it again, and the second failure looks like new information.

**Say whether something was verified or assumed.** A guess recorded as a fact is worse than no entry, because it will be built on.

**Write state, not narration.** "The port is read from `runtime_directory()/port`" beats "I looked for where the port comes from". Nobody needs the search; they need the answer.

**Do not restate the user's messages.** They travel through the fold word for word and are still in the conversation. Record what they *imply* for the work — a constraint that rules an approach out, a preference that settles a choice — not a paraphrase of what was already said better.

**Do not repeat what the memory below already holds.** Record only what these turns added.

When a detail is borderline, keep it. A redundant line costs a line. A lost one costs the work that produced it, paid by someone who no longer has the turns that would explain it.
